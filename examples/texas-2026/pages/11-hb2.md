ABOUT THIS SLIDE
Where the new leaders can realistically get help for Austin through a Republican Senate: money. The big number is HB 2, the 2025 school funding law. The arc is the Senate vote on it, 31 to 0. The point to land: the Senate will not repeal vouchers or the takeover rule, but it has already voted unanimously for school funding, so funding is where a Democratic governor and House can find Republican votes.
Terms: recapture is the money a property-wealthy district like Austin sends back to the state when its local property taxes exceed what the state formula says it should get.

SAY (opening)
In 2025 the Legislature passed HB 2, about 8.5 billion dollars in new money for public schools over the two-year state budget, including teacher pay and special education.

[click] Every senator voted for it, 31 to 0. That same session also created the voucher program in a separate bill, SB 2. The two officials who set that session's agenda, Governor Abbott and Lieutenant Governor Patrick, both lost this month. Senators who voted for school money under them have the same reasons to vote for it now.

[click] Austin needs that money. Before its cuts, the district projected a 181 million dollar gap for 2026-27, and it budgeted about 715 million dollars of local property taxes to send back to the state in 2025-26. That is recapture. More state funding, or a change to recapture, is the most realistic help a new governor and House can win from this Senate.

IF ASKED
Will Republican senators now drop vouchers? Nothing in the record says so. Repealing the program would need a bill through the same Senate that passed it. Funding is the area with a voting record of agreement.
Is the 181 million the current deficit? No. It is the projection before the district's reductions.
