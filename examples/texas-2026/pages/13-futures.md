ABOUT THIS SLIDE
Three paths for Austin ISD. The first column, marked "Most likely," is the petitions holding. The other two are both takeovers, and the slide's second argument is that they would be very different: one run by a pro-public-school TEA under a commissioner Hinojosa appoints, the other run by Morath's TEA, the same agency that has run Houston's takeover since 2023. Even the worse case for Austin, a takeover, would look different under the new administration.
"Most likely" is a judgment from the petition record, with no probability behind it. Say so plainly.

SAY (opening)
The likeliest path runs through the parent petitions. Parents at Burnet and Webb petitioned to close the two schools, and the superintendent reported that initial counts topped the required majority. If the petitions hold, the commissioner generally has to order closure, and the elected board keeps the district. They still need to be validated by December 1, and TEA is reviewing a complaint about signatures at Webb. Closing two schools is a real loss for those families.

[click] If the petitions fail, the remaining remedy is a state-appointed board. Morath may still be in office to sign that order. But once Hinojosa's commissioner is confirmed, the law lets that commissioner replace any or all of the managers, so the takeover would end up run by her administration's people.

[click] That makes this a very different prospect from a takeover under the previous administration. Hinojosa, a former Austin ISD trustee, has pledged to end state takeovers. A commissioner she picks would be expected to work with the district to improve the two schools and hand control back to elected trustees. Under Morath, Houston's state board has been in place since June 2023, now extended through June 2027, with a model imposed from the top.

IF ASKED
Would a Hinojosa takeover definitely be better? It is a scenario, not a promise. A new commissioner is still bound by state law, confirmation needs at least nine Republican votes, and until then Morath stays in office.
