# Texas Public Education After the 2026 Election
## What changed, what didn't, and what it means for Austin ISD

**Audience:** Austin residents, parents, educators, and community members with no assumed knowledge of Texas government.

**Central argument:** The 2026 election creates the strongest opportunity in decades to change Texas public education policy. But divided legislative control and existing state laws mean the immediate consequences for Austin ISD will depend on legal deadlines, Senate cooperation, appointments, and the choices made by the Texas Education Agency.

**Evidence convention:** Confirmed (election returns, statutes, official documents, direct statements). Pending (unresolved administrative decision, certification, appointment, or legislative action). Scenario (analytical projection).

## Slide 1: Texas Voted for Change. What Happens to Our Schools?

- Texas elected a new governor with a public education-focused platform. Several other statewide offices also changed hands. Austin ISD faces a possible state intervention during the transition. The central question: how much can the new government actually change?
- Notes: Gina Hinojosa previously served as an Austin ISD trustee and board president. Two overlapping stories: a statewide political change, and an immediate legal problem facing AISD.
- Sources: Texas Tribune, Hinojosa's victory and AISD: https://www.texastribune.org/2026/11/04/texas-gina-hinojosa-austin-isd-tea-takeover/

## Slide 2: The Election Changed Almost Everything, Except the Senate

| Position | Election outcome | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Governor | Gina Hinojosa (D) | Executive leadership, appointments, vetoes |
| Lieutenant Governor | Vikki Goodwin (D) | Presides over Senate |
| Attorney General | Nathan Johnson (D) | State legal strategy |
| Comptroller | Sarah Eckhardt (D) | Revenue estimates, financial oversight, vouchers |
| Texas House | Democratic majority | Can advance education legislation |
| Texas Senate | 19 Republicans, 12 Democrats | Can block legislation and confirmations |

- Notes: not a unified Democratic government. The lieutenant governor traditionally controls much of the Senate agenda, but senators can change the rules that grant those powers. Avoid calling results certified until the canvass is complete.
- Sources: Texas SOS returns https://electionresults.sos.state.tx.us/results.html ; Texas Tribune, Republican Senate majority https://www.texastribune.org/2026/11/05/texas-senate-republican-majority-lieutenant-governor-powers/

## Slide 3: Who Actually Runs Texas Public Education?

- Legislature writes school laws and determines funding. Governor shapes priorities, appoints officials, signs or vetoes bills. TEA administers education laws and oversees school performance. State Board of Education sets curriculum standards. Local school boards govern their districts unless state intervention suspends their authority.
- Notes: the education commissioner is appointed, not elected, and makes many of the specific intervention decisions. The Legislature writes the rulebook, TEA administers it, the district operates the schools.
- Sources: Texas Education Code ch. 7 https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/ED/htm/ED.7.htm ; ch. 39A https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/ED/htm/ED.39A.htm

## Slide 4: Why Austin ISD Is Facing a State Takeover

- Burnet and Webb middle schools received fifth consecutive unacceptable performance ratings. AISD has appealed. If the ratings are finalized, state law requires a consequential intervention. A problem at two campuses could result in replacement of the entire district's elected board.
- Notes: the trigger applies to campuses, but one required remedy operates at the district level. Do not imply all AISD schools are failing or that AISD has already been taken over.
- Sources: AISD superintendent's September 2026 update https://www.austinisd.org/announcements/2026/09/14/message-from-superintendent-segura ; TEC §39A.111 (ch. 39A link above)

## Slide 5: AISD Has Problems a Change in Governor Won't Fix Overnight

- Declining enrollment reduces revenue. Staffing, transportation, operating costs remain high. Formulas don't align with district costs. Recapture sends substantial local property-tax revenue into the statewide system.
- Notes: AISD's adopted 2025–26 budget estimated about $715.5 million in recapture payments. For 2026–27, AISD originally faced a projected $181 million shortfall and approved major reductions; its June 2026 budget included an $887 million operating budget. The $181 million is a pre-reduction gap, not a current deficit.
- Sources: https://www.austinisd.org/budget ; https://www.austinisd.org/budget/process ; https://www.austinisd.org/announcements/2026/06/23/superintendents-update-approved-2026-27-budget-en-espanol

## Slide 6: Five F Ratings: What the Law Actually Says

- §39A.111 requires action after five consecutive unacceptable campus ratings: appoint a districtwide board of managers, or close the campus. Parent petitions and other procedures affect which is selected.
- Notes: mandatory language. A new commissioner can exercise judgment within the law, but refusing to implement invites legal challenge. A monitor or conservator alone does not satisfy this five-year requirement.
- Sources: TEC §§39A.111–39A.112

## Slide 7: December Is a Milestone, Not Necessarily the Final Decision

- Dec 1, 2026: reported deadline for validating and submitting parent petitions. Around Dec 10: expected resolution of rating appeals. Dec 15: reported opportunity for AISD trustees to request a different action. Jan 19, 2027: expected inauguration. Feb 15, 2027: reported order deadline in the campus-closure petition process.
- Notes: distinguish administrative deadlines from when an order takes effect. Some decisions could be made under one administration and some under the next. Verify each deadline again before presenting.
- Sources: Austin Politics timeline https://www.austinpolitics.org/on-my-block/webb-burnet-petition-review-aisd-outside-operators-dobie-linder-pecan-springs ; Texas Constitution art. IV https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CN/htm/CN.4.htm

## Slide 8: Parents Have a Voice, But Their Options Are Difficult

- A qualifying petition from parents of a majority of enrolled students can request a statutory action. The commissioner generally must follow a valid petition, subject to an exception for a school board request. Burnet and Webb petitions have generated disagreement. Closing a campus may preserve district governance but disrupt families.
- Notes: under §39A.112 the elected board may request a different action; the commissioner may grant it. The Webb PTA raised concerns about petition gathering: allegations under review, not established misconduct.
- Sources: TEC §39A.112 ; Austin Politics link above

## Slide 9: A TEA Intervention Can Mean Several Very Different Things

| Intervention | What changes? |
|---|---|
| Monitor | Observes and reports |
| Conservator | Oversees designated functions |
| Management team | Directs specified operations |
| Campus closure | Ends the campus's operation |
| Board of managers | Replaces the elected board's authority |

- Notes: §39A.003 requires review of a conservator or management team at least every 90 days. A board of managers suspends the elected board's powers and the commissioner appoints a superintendent.
- Sources: TEC §§39A.002–39A.003, 39A.201–39A.209

## Slide 10: Hinojosa's Education Philosophy

- Increase investment and teacher pay. Preserve local control. Replace Commissioner Mike Morath. Oppose vouchers, reform testing. Move away from punitive takeovers.
- Notes: she publicly promised to replace Morath on day one. Eliminating STAAR or the takeover framework requires more than a governor's authority.
- Sources: Texas Tribune AISD link above ; https://www.texastribune.org/2026/11/05/texas-dems-vowed-seismic-education-change-heres-how-schools-could-change-for-millions-of-students/

## Slide 11: Can Hinojosa Really Replace the TEA Commissioner on Day One?

- The governor nominates the commissioner, who serves a four-year term aligned with the governor's. Senate confirmation is required. Removal of an incumbent is restricted. A holdover may remain until a successor qualifies.
- Notes: TEC §§7.051–7.053 govern appointment, term and removal. Constitution art. IV §12 generally requires two-thirds of senators present for gubernatorial appointments. Art. XV §9 sets a two-thirds requirement for removal by the appointing governor, which raises an issue when the incoming governor did not appoint the incumbent. Art. XVI §17: officers continue until successors qualify. A consensus nominee, a vacancy or a lawful recess appointment could change timelines. Do not state Morath's removal on Jan 19 is legally guaranteed.
- Sources: ch. 7 link ; art. IV ; art. XV https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CN/htm/CN.15.htm ; art. XVI https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CN/htm/CN.16.htm

## Slide 12: If AISD Is Taken Over, Can a New Commissioner Undo It?

- Before an order the commissioner may have choices. After an order the intervention is evaluated under the statute. The commissioner may replace individual managers. Early board removal has special conditions. Conservatorship has different rules.
- Notes: §39A.207 lets the commissioner replace board-of-managers members. §39A.209 restricts early removal of the board, including a requirement tied to two consecutive acceptable ratings at the triggering campus. §39A.208 governs expiration and phased return of elected trustees. Changing who runs a takeover is easier than ending it.
- Sources: TEC §§39A.207–39A.209

## Slide 13: The Governor Has Powerful Tools, Even Without New Laws

- Executive tools: appointments and agency leadership; policy direction within existing authority; budget proposals and negotiation; vetoes and special-session agendas.
- Requires the Legislature: repealing the five-F rule; replacing statutory testing; changing finance formulas; repealing the voucher program.
- Sources: art. IV ; TEC ch. 7 and 39A

## Slide 14: The House Can Start the Reform Process

- Bill filed, committee, floor via House leadership, then Senate; both chambers must agree on the same text before it reaches the governor. Passing the House is not passing a law.
- Sources: https://www.house.texas.gov/about ; https://capitol.texas.gov/

## Slide 15: The Senate Is Still the Biggest Roadblock

- 19 Republicans, 12 Democrats. The lieutenant governor traditionally influences committee assignments and scheduling; Senate rules grant those powers and Republican senators can vote to change them. Appointments face a two-thirds threshold.
- Sources: Texas Tribune Senate link above ; art. IV §12

## Slide 16: A Friendly Attorney General Changes the Legal Environment

- Represents the state, issues legal opinions, sets litigation priorities. Cannot repeal education statutes or order TEA to cancel a takeover. Under Ken Paxton the office frequently pursued legal action involving school district policies.
- Sources: Texas Tribune education outlook link above

## Slide 17: The Comptroller Controls More Than the State's Checkbook

- Estimates revenue available to the Legislature, certifies appropriations fit, administers the Texas Education Freedom Accounts program created by SB 2 (2025). Cannot terminate a program the Legislature established.
- Sources: art. III §49a https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CN/htm/CN.3.htm ; https://comptroller.texas.gov/transparency/reports/biennial-revenue-estimate/ ; https://capitol.texas.gov/billlookup/BillSummary.aspx?Bill=SB2&LegSess=89R

## Slide 18: The State Board of Education Could Shift, Too

- 15 elected members. Democrats appear positioned for an 8–7 majority. A close District 8 result remains subject to final counting and possible recount. The board does not choose whether AISD gets a board of managers.
- Sources: https://www.texastribune.org/2026/11/04/texas-state-board-education-elections-2026/

## Slide 19: Can Republicans Strip Power Before Hinojosa Takes Office?

- Rep. Brian Harrison called for a special session before inauguration to limit gubernatorial emergency powers; only the sitting governor can call one. Senate Republicans can change chamber rules without passing a law. As of Nov 6 these are proposals, not enacted changes.
- Sources: https://gmg-kprc-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/local/2026/11/05/hinojosa-responds-to-republican-calls-to-limit-governors-powers-before-she-takes-office/ ; Texas Tribune Senate link above

## Slide 20: Bipartisan Education Reform Is More Realistic Than It Sounds

- Plausible agreement: teacher pay, special education, rural district stability, CTE, early literacy and safety. Contentious: repealing vouchers, eliminating accountability laws, redesigning testing, removing takeover authority.
- Notes: HB 2 (2025): about $8.5 billion in additional public school funding; the Senate passed the negotiated package unanimously.
- Sources: https://www.senate.texas.gov/news.php?id=20250523a ; https://capitol.texas.gov/billlookup/BillSummary.aspx?Bill=HB2&LegSess=89R ; https://www.texastribune.org/2025/06/04/texas-public-education-schools-funding-bill-explained/

## Slide 21: Houston ISD Shows Both the Promise and Danger of Takeovers

- Gains: improved ratings at numerous campuses, expanded supports, higher pay in some programs. Costs: significant enrollment losses, higher teacher turnover, increased instructional spending, reduced local control.
- Notes: TEA took over HISD in 2023. A September 2026 Texas Tribune analysis reported about 21,000 students lost since the takeover began. Avoid a simple success-or-failure verdict.
- Sources: https://www.uh.edu/news-events/stories/2026/january/01152026-houston-isd-takeover-by-the-numbers.php ; https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/21/houston-isd-state-takeover-sustainability/

## Slide 22: Could an AISD Takeover Under Hinojosa Actually Help?

- A constructive intervention would need: locally trusted leadership, transparent goals, targeted support, financially sustainable plans, protection against disruption, a credible path back to elected governance.

## Slide 23: Three Plausible Futures for AISD

| | Best case | Working expectation | Worst case |
|---|---|---|---|
| TEA leadership | Cooperative commissioner confirmed | Delayed or negotiated transition | Prolonged leadership conflict |
| AISD intervention | Narrower lawful remedy; local control largely retained | Intervention proceeds with modified priorities | Districtwide board replacement with prolonged disruption |
| School finances | New targeted support and legislative relief | Continued pressure with incremental help | Further cuts and enrollment losses |
| Community trust | Strong engagement | Mixed confidence | Deep conflict |
| Student outcomes | Sustained improvement | Uneven progress | Disruption outweighs gains |

- Conditional scenarios, not probabilities.

## Slide 24: What Should Austin Watch Next?

1. Final Burnet and Webb ratings and petition determinations.
2. Whether TEA issues an order before or after inauguration.
3. The commissioner transition and Senate confirmation.
4. The Senate's 2027 rules and education committee leadership.
5. Early legislative proposals on funding, accountability and vouchers.

Closing statement (keep): "The election changed who holds power. The next question is whether those officials can use that power, within the laws that still exist, to make Texas public schools stronger."

Other sources: https://www.austinisd.org/ ; https://tea.texas.gov/
