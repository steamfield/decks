ABOUT THIS SLIDE
Three paths for Austin ISD. The first column, marked "Most likely," is the petitions holding. The other two are both takeovers, and the slide's second argument is that they would not be the same: one run by a commissioner Hinojosa picks, one run the way Houston's has been.
"Most likely" is a judgment from the petition record, with no probability behind it. Say so plainly.

SAY (opening)
The likeliest path runs through the parent petitions. Parents at Burnet and Webb petitioned to close the two schools, and the superintendent reported that initial counts topped the required majority. If the petitions hold, the commissioner generally has to order closure, and the elected board keeps the district. They still need to be validated by December 1, and TEA is reviewing a complaint about signatures at Webb. Closing two schools is a real loss for those families.

[click] If the petitions fail, the remaining remedy is a state-appointed board. Morath may sign that order, but a commissioner Hinojosa appoints could replace those managers later.

[click] Who runs a takeover shapes what it aims for. Hinojosa has pledged to end state takeovers. A commissioner she picks would be expected to work with the district toward returning control to elected trustees. Houston shows the other version: a state board in place since June 2023, extended through June 2027, run from the top down.

IF ASKED
Would a Hinojosa takeover definitely be better? It is a scenario, not a promise. A new commissioner is still bound by state law, and confirmation needs Republican votes.
